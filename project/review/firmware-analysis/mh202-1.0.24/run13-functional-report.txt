## Delimiter order and why a later finalizer can skip

At the supplied final delimiter, OR finalization is considered first (`0x632c..0x634c`): OR-seen must be nonzero, accumulator!=`n`, pending==`n`. The chosen mixed paths satisfy those predicates after their second separator. Selected constant final-clause evaluation at`0x6ac4` stores pending at`0x6acc`; actual OR call`0x6b00` combines normalized accumulator/pending and stores accumulator at`0x6b08`.

PARENTESI then performs its previously reviewed buffer clears/normalization to left0/1, operator`=`, right`1`. Crucially this **final** OR block does not reset pending to`n`; that byte retains the final clause's0/1. This differs from an intermediate repeated-OR boundary, whose pending reset is explicit.

The subsequent AND gate at`0x6bfc..0x6c1c` also requires AND-seen!=0, accumulator!=`n`, **pending==`n`**. Its branch`0x6c1c` returns at`0x748c` when pending is not`n`. Consequently, after admitted final OR evaluation on these mixed paths, the AND flag can remain1 while its finalizer skips. No later AND helper or second normalization is reached in those selected states.

For the AND-only control case, OR-seen stays0, so OR finalization is bypassed. Pending remains`n` after the intermediate AND combine; the AND gate admits its selected final comparison at`0x7394`, stores pending at`0x739c`, calls AND at`0x73d0` and stores the result at`0x73d8`, then normalizes the output buffers. This is a concrete counter-route confirming that the final AND block exists and is conditionally skipped rather than globally absent.
